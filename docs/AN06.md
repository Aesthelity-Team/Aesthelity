# AN-06 layering explanation

Issue #26 adds general category guidance on Home and Routine analysis. The suggested sequence is cleanser, serum or treatment, moisturizer, then daytime sunscreen. Each row explains its position. Product labels and clinician instructions take priority.

The general category sequence follows the American Academy of Dermatology’s application-order guidance. Grouping serums with leave-on treatment is an application convention. Formula-specific directions can override it.

Source accessed November 18, 2026
[American Academy of Dermatology application order](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/apply-skin-care-certain-order)

## Inputs and uncertainty

New routine snapshots retain the selected product category. Existing snapshots without a category decode successfully and remain uncertain. Ingredient list coverage must be complete and nonempty. Manual lists retain their unverified completeness status.

Toner, mist, mask, missing categories, missing snapshots, incomplete lists, and nighttime sunscreen have no rule in this version. If any step lacks enough information, the entire routine remains in its saved order and displays uncertainty. Empty routines also remain uncertain.

Within a category, the saved relative order is retained and the unknown relative order is disclosed. Multiple serums or treatments display a caution that their combined use and relative order need product-specific instructions.

## Contract boundaries

Guidance retains each routine step ID and never edits the saved routine. Ingredient compatibility findings, recorded allergies, and the backend AN-02/AI analysis contract retain their existing behavior. A suggested sequence cannot resolve a caution, allergen finding, or incomplete assessment. General layering guidance is available on-device before the separate backend compatibility assessment.
